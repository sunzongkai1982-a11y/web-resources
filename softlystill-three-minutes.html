Is Three Minutes Enough? Choosing Session Length Without Guilt

A large number of people do not practise at all because the only version they have encountered is a twenty-minute session they do not have twenty minutes for. Duration is the wrong thing to optimise first.

What duration actually changes

Longer sessions do not produce proportionally better results. What tends to happen across the first few minutes is that attention settles; what happens across twenty is that you get further through whatever the practice is doing. Those are different effects, and the first one is available quickly.

This is why short sessions are not a compromise version. Three minutes of settled attention is a complete session — just a shorter reach. A practice to steady yourself before something specific does not need twenty minutes, and insisting on twenty means it does not happen.

Frequency beats duration

The effect most people are looking for — being less hijacked by reaction — comes from repetition, not from occasional intensity. Three minutes most days moves considerably more than one long session a week, for the same reason that daily walking beats a monthly hike.

The practical implication for anyone starting: pick the shortest session you can honestly do every day, and only lengthen it once that is automatic.

Short sessions worth knowing exist

These exist precisely because real practice happens in awkward gaps, not in a quiet room with a cushion:

  • Before an interview. Three minutes of grounding where the issue is self-doubt rather than nerves — beside the building, three minutes.
  • At your desk. Three minutes without leaving it, for days when leaving the desk is not happening.
  • Already in bed, not asleep. A three-minute session aimed at that specific failure mode, rather than fighting it.
  • On a plane. Five minutes of letting go, for fear that has a definite duration anyway — fear of flying.

When the longer ones earn their keep

There are states that genuinely need room, and shorter versions of them feel like being interrupted:

  • Midday collapse. Fatigue is not the same as tension, and it takes longer to shift: seven minutes for fatigue.
  • Before something that needs your head. A fifteen-minute concentration practice before an appointment of consequence.
  • A mind that will not stop at night. The body scans built for that are deliberately long: twenty minutes in the evening.

The only rule worth keeping
