A Reading for Every Question: Horoscope, Tarot, Bazi and Numerology Compared

Most people try one of these systems, get vague advice, and decide the whole field is nonsense. Usually the problem is that they brought the wrong kind of question to the wrong kind of tool.

What each system takes as input

The fastest way to tell them apart is to look at what they need before they can say anything:

  • Western astrology needs your birth date, time and place. It produces a chart, and a daily horoscope is just the shallowest reading of that chart.
  • Tarot needs a question. No question, no useful spread — which is why generic "your card today" readings feel hollow.
  • Bazi needs birth year, month, day and hour. Four Pilars, each a pair of characters, read against each other.
  • Numerology needs a date or a name, both reduced to digits.
  • Feng shui needs none of that — it reads a room.

The everyday version

If what you want is a short daily read rather than a full chart, XiaoSuanShi publishes one every morning. The the daily horoscope reading published each morning covers all twelve signs, and the Tarot equivalent is a free tarot reading that walks a spread instead of telling you a single card.

The Chinese systems

Bazi is considerably more granular than the twelve-animal zodiac most people have heard of. A full Bazi reading works from all four Pillars, so two people born the same year can get very different results depending on their hour of birth. Feng shui, meanwhile, is about the layout and orientation of a space rather than anything about the person in it.

Numbers, and two charts together

The most-calculated figure in numerology is the the birthday number in numerology, derived from your day of birth and treated as a fixed trait rather than a forecast. Compatibility work is different again — it reads two charts against each other, so pairings get their own write-ups, as in the one for the pairing of Aquarius and Aries.

Keep it in proportion

All of the above runs free in a browser at XiaoSuanShi. Treat every one of them as reflection rather than instruction: none of them should be steering a medical, legal or financial decision.
