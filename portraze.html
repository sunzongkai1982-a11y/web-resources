What Recruiters See: A Practical Guide to Getting a Professional Headshot

A professional headshot is one of the few photos where boring beats interesting. The brief is narrow: look like yourself, on a good day, at a size that survives being cropped into a circle.

The four things that actually matter

  1. Even light on the face. A window at 45 degrees will beat an expensive camera in bad light every time. Avoid overhead office light, which puts shadows under the eyes.
  2. Focus on the eyes. Everything else can be slightly soft; the eyes cannot.
  3. Square shoulders to the camera. Angling away reads as evasive.
  4. A backdrop that does not compete. A plain wall, or a background blurred enough to be anonymous.

Dress for the room you are walking into

The formal-to-approachable line moves by profession, and getting it wrong is the most common mistake. Legal and finance lean formal and restrained — which is why headshots for lawyers sit at the conservative end with dark suiting and a neutral background. Healthcare needs to read as calm and competent rather than aloof; see the notes on headshots for doctors and for nurses. Sales-facing roles want the opposite: warmth first. That shapes both realtor headshots and dentist headshots, which are clinical environments but not clinical relationships.

At senior level the constraint flips again: an executive headshot has to work at annual-report scale, where fabric texture and retouching mistakes become visible.

Where AI generation fits

Generating a headshot from photos you already have removes the two real barriers — cost and scheduling. Portraze does exactly that, producing consistent lighting and backdrop without a studio booking. Nobody benefits from a photo that plausibly resembles no one in particular.

Counting the real cost

Booking a photographer is rarely just the session fee: there is travel, the retouching licence, and often a reshoot. Before deciding, it is worth running the numbers in the headshot cost calculator. One piece of housekeeping

Keep the full-resolution original. A crop that suits a round avatar will crop terribly for a wide banner, and regenerating from scratch in a year is far more annoying than keeping one good source file.
