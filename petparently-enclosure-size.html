The Enclosure That Meets the Size Chart Is Still Too Small

Minimum enclosure sizes get quoted a lot, and they get quoted as if hitting the number settles the question. It does not. A chart is a floor — the smallest thing that is not overtly cruel — and a cage can clear every dimension on it and still be wrong for the animal in it.

Why the floor is not the target

Length and width on a chart tell you whether an animal can turn around and take a few steps. They say nothing about whether the enclosure can hold two different climates at once, and that is the actual requirement for most reptiles and many small mammals. A gradient needs distance. If the enclosure is short, the warm side bleeds into the cool side, and the animal ends up living at whatever temperature the middle happens to be.

That is the practical argument for buying above the chart: not comfort in the abstract, but the ability to fit a warm end, a cool end and a retreat that is genuinely in the cool end. The housing guidance is built around that idea rather than around dimensions alone.

The retreat is not decoration

A hide is often treated as enrichment — something to add once the basics are covered. For a prey species it is closer to the basics themselves. An animal with nowhere to be unseen is under a low-grade stress all day, and that shows up as refusal to feed and as pacing long before it shows up as anything a vet can diagnose. Two hides, one at each end, so the animal never has to choose between feeling safe and being at the right temperature.

Space to use matters as much as floor area, which is the argument the enrichment pages keep making: climbing height, digging depth and things to move around are what turn square inches into somewhere to live. A gecko kept on a flat floor in a correctly sized tank is still under-housed — the leopard gecko write-up is a clear case of a species whose needs are vertical as much as horizontal.

The honest test

Can the animal be at the warm end and out of sight at the same time? Can it be at the cool end and out of sight at the same time? If either answer is no, the enclosure is too small regardless of what the chart said.
