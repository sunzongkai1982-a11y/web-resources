Rolling Out Headshots Across a Team Without It Taking a Month

Team headshot projects almost never fail on photography. They fail on logistics — the scheduler who waits for the one person on sabbatical, and the resulting grid where fourteen people look like they were photographed in different decades.

Why these projects stall

The default plan is to book one photographer for one afternoon and get everyone in the room. In practice there is always a fifth of the team unavailable, so the shoot happens twice, with slightly different light, and the "about us" page quietly becomes evidence of that.

The alternative is to stop treating it as an event. If the constraint is scheduling rather than cost, generating portraits from photos people already have removes the hard part entirely — that is what team headshots are set up to do, and it is why the resulting set is consistent by construction rather than by retouching.

The three variables that make a grid look coherent

Consistency is not about everyone wearing the same thing. It is about three things being held steady:

  1. Backdrop. The single most visible variable when images sit side by side. One neutral value, no gradient, no plausible-looking office blur.
  2. Light direction and softness. Faces should be lit from roughly the same angle across the set. This is what separates a real set from a collage.
  3. Crop and framing. Decide headroom and shoulder width once, then apply it to everyone. A grid where eye lines sit at different heights reads as careless even when every individual photo is fine.

How much latitude each role gets

Uniformity taken too far is its own failure — a team of creatives looking like an audit firm. The formal-to-approachable line legitimately moves by function, and it is worth making those differences deliberate rather than accidental:

  • Engineering reads best relaxed and unbothered; see software engineer headshots.
  • Client-facing advisory — consultants in client-facing advisory roles and financial advisors — needs restrained credibility, which mostly means darker suiting and nothing competing in the background.
  • Sales wants the opposite end: visible warmth. The notes on sales manager headshots lean into approachability explicitly.

Questions you will get asked, in advance

Someone will ask whether the photo is "really them", someone will ask about licensing, and someone will ask whether existing images can be reused. Decide all three before you send the first email — most of them are answered in the the frequently asked questions section, along with the usual input requirements.

The tedious parts worth doing properly

Keep the full-resolution original for every person. Round avatar crops ignore horizontal usage entirely, so a set that was generated only as squares will be regenerated from scratch the first time anyone needs a wide banner. Working files belong alongside the delivered images, not in someone's downloads folder — the tools page and the the editor in your browser cover the resizing and export side.

If you need several variants per person rather than one — different backgrounds for different surfaces — do it as a series so the set stays matched. Longer-term background reading sits in the guides.
