The Work People Do on Exam Morning Is Usually Too Late

Before anything important, most people attempt to stop being nervous. It rarely works, and the attempt often makes it worse — because you are now nervous and failing at something else too.

Arousal is not the enemy

The relationship between arousal and performance is not linear: some improves it, and too much degrades it. Overwrought before a presentation is not a sign you are unprepared; it is your body allocating resources. Trying to reach zero is aiming at the wrong target — flat and under-aroused performs worse than moderately keyed up.

The practical consequence is that the goal is not calm. It is keeping it somewhere you can use, which is a much more achievable ask and why grounding tends to outperform relaxation here. There is a twelve-minute session specifically for test anxiety, deliberately placed in the mid-afternoon rather than the final ten minutes.

The morning-of session has a narrow job

This is where most people mis-time things. A long calming session an hour before, intended to undo weeks of tension, has one session's worth of force against several weeks — which is why it feels ineffective and adds a new worry.

What morning-of work can genuinely do is shorten the worst stretch and give you something to do with your hands. Short and specific. A seven-minute breathing session for exam day is scoped to exactly that, and the longer preparatory version — twenty minutes for anticipatory anxiety — belongs to the days beforehand, where it has room to do something.

Irritability is a different signal

Sometimes what gets labelled nerves is not nerves at all. Irritation — at colleagues, the task, the unfairness of being assessed — does not respond to calming, because there is nothing to settle. There is momentum, and it wants somewhere to go.

Noting suits this because it does not argue with the feeling; it names and moves on. Two available versions: a short one on a break, and a longer one for before a presentation, which is the case where it matters most — nobody wants to walk into a room already annoyed.

The honest version

Being nervous beforehand is close to universal among people who care about the outcome, and the people who appear calm are frequently either very practised or not trying especially hard. Every session here is free in a browser at SoftlyStill.
