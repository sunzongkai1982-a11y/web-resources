The Twenty Minutes Afterwards Are Worth More Than the Ten Minutes During

Every piece of guidance about staying calm in an argument fails the same way: the moment it would be useful is precisely the moment you are least capable of using it. Trying anyway tends to add a second failure on top of the first.

During is not the window

In the middle of a row, a shock, or a genuinely bad afternoon, the part of you that would deliberately settle your attention is not the part driving. Redirecting it is not a technique problem; it is a physics problem. People who conclude they are bad at this have usually only ever practised at the hardest possible moment.

The window that genuinely works opens later — five to twenty minutes after, when the event is over but your body has not caught up. Heart still going, shoulders up, thoughts circling. Aware enough to choose something, not yet settled on its own. That is where practice has purchase, and it is a legitimate time to use it rather than a consolation prize.

Match the practice to what actually happened

Afterwards is not one state, and treating it as one is the other common mistake:

An argument

What lingers is usually a body in a defensive posture rather than the content of the disagreement. Grounding rather than analysis — there is a short grounding session built for exactly this, which is deliberately only five minutes because longer is not going to happen.

A shock

Different texture: flat, slowed, sometimes oddly detached. Grief-adjacent states need room rather than focus, and letting-go practice fits better. There is a ten-minute version written for sadness following a shock.

Anger that arrives later

Notably, anger frequently shows up once the adrenaline has passed — or at three in the morning, replaying the conversation properly this time. Interrupting before it consolidates matters more than trying to relax. A three-minute session for that specific situation exists because it recurs often enough to deserve one.

A bad day that was nobody's fault

When nothing dramatic happened and you are simply scraped raw, the usual recommendation is one of the last things anyone wants to hear — and yet it is the one with evidence behind it. There is a longer one for this: fifteen minutes of gratitude work after a bad day.

Not Quite a Rule

Doing this twenty minutes late is not doing it wrong. It is doing it when it can work. All sessions are free in a browser at SoftlyStill.
