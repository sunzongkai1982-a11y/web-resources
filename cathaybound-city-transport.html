Getting Around a Chinese City Without Losing Your Mornings

Chinese metros are excellent and visitors routinely budget them wrongly. The mistake is comparing journey time against driving time at home — the variable that actually eats your day is walking distance, and it is invisible on every map app until you are standing in it.

The line-by-line time is not the journey time

A fifteen-minute trip can involve ten minutes underground getting to the platform, because interchange corridors in the larger networks are frequently several hundred metres long. Multiply that across three changes on a route that looked like half an hour, and the afternoon reorganises itself.

Second small thing that costs real time: station exits are numbered, sometimes with twenty-odd of them, and choosing incorrectly can deposit you several blocks away on the wrong side of a major road. Checking which exit before you surface takes fifteen seconds and saves ten minutes. All of this is set out properly under metro, taxi and transport apps.

When to take the taxi anyway

Generally: short distances outside peak hours, door-to-door to somewhere awkward, or anything with luggage. The metro's advantage — predictable time regardless of traffic — stops being decisive below about four or five kilometres, where you would spend longer walking to and through stations than riding.

Peak hours change the calculation entirely. The metro becomes uncomfortable and the taxi becomes slow, and neither is wrong; rush hour is simply expensive in whichever currency you choose. Budget for it rather than discovering it.

Two navigation problems to expect

Positioning drifts in dense city centres. Tall buildings interfere enough that your blue dot can be twenty metres off, which matters most when you are looking for a specific entrance rather than a street. Cross-checking against the street name is faster than waiting for it to settle.

The other is transliteration — the same place has several plausible spellings in Latin script, and the one locals use may not be the one your map shows. If something will not come up, ask rather than searching harder.

The choice that shapes everything downstream

Whichever app you set up in the first hour, for ride-hailing and metro ticketing alike, will be the one you use for the whole trip — so it is worth doing properly once, with hotel wi-fi, rather than improvising at a station.
